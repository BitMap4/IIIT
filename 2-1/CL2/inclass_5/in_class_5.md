### Key Patterns in Selectional Restrictions:

1. Agency Requirements
   - Many verbs require conscious, intentional agents
   - Helps eliminate inanimate or non-sentient antecedents

2. Professional Capability
   - Domain-specific verbs restrict antecedents to those with relevant skills/roles
   - Particularly evident in sports, entertainment, and political contexts

3. Cognitive Capacity
   - Verbs of thinking, deciding, and perceiving require mentally capable agents
   - Automatically excludes inanimate or non-conscious antecedents

4. Role-based Restrictions
   - Institutional or professional verbs limit possible antecedents to role-holders
   - Creates strong expectations for antecedent identification

5. Action-Specific Requirements
   - Physical actions require capable agents
   - Professional actions require qualified agents
   - Social actions require socially capable agents

These selectional restrictions serve as powerful constraints in antecedent identification, often working alongside other linguistic features to establish correct coreference relationships.
